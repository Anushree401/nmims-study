1. In-Depth Theory & Lecture Explanation

Faithful to Video processing.pptx & Syllabus

1.1 Spatial Redundancy vs. Temporal Redundancy (From NMIMS Paper 1 Q1d)

  • Spatial Redundancy: Correlation between adjacent pixels *within the same frame*. Pixels in a blue sky patch have almost identical values. Exploited via Intra-frame compression (DCT transform coding, quantization).
  • Temporal Redundancy: Correlation between pixels *across consecutive time frames*. In 30 fps video, background scenery remains virtually identical from frame \(t\) to frame \(t+1\); only moving foreground objects shift position. Exploited via Inter-frame compression (Motion Estimation and Motion Compensation).

1.2 Video Frame Classifications: I, P, and B Frames

Frame Type Encoding Mechanism & Dependency Compression Ratio
I-Frame (Intra-coded) Self-contained; encoded independently of all other frames like a standalone JPEG image. Acts as a random access anchor and recovers from transmission errors. Lowest (Largest file size: \(1\times\)).
P-Frame (Predictive-coded) Forward predicted from the closest preceding I-frame or P-frame. Encodes only motion vectors and residual error differences. Medium (\(\approx 3\times\) smaller than I-frame).
B-Frame (Bi-directionally predictive) Interpolated bidirectionally using both past and future reference frames. Highest compression efficiency. Highest (\(\approx 6\times\) smaller than I-frame).

1.3 Block-Matching Motion Estimation Algorithms

Each current frame is divided into macroblocks of size \(N \times N\) (typically \(16 \times 16\)). For each block, the encoder searches within a search window of parameter \(p\) in the reference frame to find the best match minimizing the Mean Absolute Difference (MAD):

$$\mathbf{\text{MAD}(dx, dy) = \frac{1}{N^2} \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} |f_t(x+i, y+j) - f_{t-1}(x+i+dx, y+j+dy)|}$$

1. Exhaustive Block Matching Algorithm (EBMA / Full Search):

Evaluates every single candidate position in the search window \([-p, +p]\).
Total candidate locations: \(\mathbf{(2p + 1)^2}\).
Property: Provably optimal (finds absolute global minimum); exceptionally high computational burden.

2. Three-Step Search (TSS) Algorithm:

Fast search with logarithmic step size reduction:

  1. Step 1: Set initial step size \(S = \lceil p / 2 \rceil = 4\) (for \(p = 7\)). Test center point and 8 surrounding points at distance \(\pm S\). Pick point with minimum MAD.
  2. Step 2: Halve step size \(S = S / 2 = 2\). Center new search at the winner from Step 1; evaluate 8 surrounding points.
  3. Step 3: Halve step size \(S = 1\). Evaluate 8 surrounding points at distance \(\pm 1\). The minimum is the final Motion Vector!

Total evaluations in TSS: \(9 + 8 + 8 = \mathbf{25\text{ locations}}\) (compared to \((2\cdot 7 + 1)^2 = \mathbf{225\text{ locations}}\) in Full Search: 9× faster!).

1.4 Two-Dimensional Logarithmic Search

Two-Dimensional Logarithmic Search (TDL) reduces motion-search cost by testing the center and points at a coarse step around it, moving the center to the best match, and reducing the step size when the best match is the center. When an off-center point wins, recentre and continue at the current step. Once the step reaches one pixel and the center is best, check the final neighboring points to select the minimum-cost motion vector. Boundary handling and exact point patterns vary by presentation of the algorithm.

MethodSearch strategyStrength / limitation
Exhaustive block matchingEvaluates every candidate displacement in the search window.Finds the global minimum within the window; highest computation.
2D logarithmic searchCoarse-to-fine logarithmic step reduction around successive best locations.Fewer evaluations; may miss the global minimum on irregular cost surfaces.
Three-Step SearchTests a fixed nine-point pattern, halves step size, and repeats.Predictable low computation; can miss the global minimum.

All methods typically minimize a block-matching cost such as MAD. Fewer candidate evaluations trade computation for a possibility of a less accurate match; fast searches are not generally guaranteed to equal exhaustive search.

Final Revision Anchor

Core Exam Takeaways:

  • Redundancy: Spatial = within frame (DCT); Temporal = across time (Motion Vectors).
  • Frame Hierarchy: I-frame (standalone) \(\to\) P-frame (forward) \(\to\) B-frame (bidirectional).
  • EBMA Candidates: \((2p + 1)^2\). For \(p = 7 \implies 225\) searches.
  • TSS Efficiency: Requires only 25 search evaluations for \(p = 7\) (\(9 + 8 + 8\)).
