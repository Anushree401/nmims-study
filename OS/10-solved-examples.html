Basic 1 | Explain a process transition

Question. A running process requests disk input. Describe its likely state path.

Reasoning. It cannot use the CPU while waiting for the device event. The OS blocks it, schedules another ready process, then moves it to Ready when the I/O completion event arrives.

Answer. Running → Blocked → Ready → Running. The event makes it ready; the scheduler/dispatcher later gives it the CPU.

Basic 2 | Calculate CPU metrics

Question. A process arrives at 2, first starts at 5, completes at 11, and uses 4 CPU units. Find response, turnaround, waiting.

Work. Response=5−2=3 units. Turnaround=11−2=9 units. Waiting=9−4=5 units (single CPU burst; no extra overhead).

Check. Waiting plus service = 5+4=9 = turnaround.

Basic 3 | Semaphore interpretation

Question. A counting semaphore protects 4 identical devices. What is its initial value and what happens when all four are allocated?

Answer. Initialize to 4. Each successful wait consumes one permit; after four acquisitions the count is 0. A fifth requester blocks until a holder signals after releasing a device.

Intermediate 2 | Banker safety

Question. Available=(1,1). P0 Allocation=(1,0), Need=(1,1); P1 Allocation=(0,1), Need=(0,1). Is the state safe?

Work. Work=(1,1). P0 fits; finish P0, Work=(1,1)+(1,0)=(2,1). P1 fits; finish P1, Work=(2,1)+(0,1)=(2,2). All processes finish.

Answer. Safe sequence ⟨P0,P1⟩. Units are resource instances; vectors are compared componentwise.

Intermediate 3 | LRU trace

Question. Two frames, references 1,2,1,3,2. Apply LRU.

Trace. 1 fault [1,-]; 2 fault [1,2]; 1 hit (1 most recent); 3 fault and evicts 2 (least recently used) giving [1,3]; 2 fault and evicts 1 (among [1,3], 1 was older) giving [2,3].

Answer. 4 faults. Check: references 2 and 1 initially load (2 faults), 1 hits, 3 replaces 2 (3), 2 replaces 1 (4).

Intermediate 4 | SCAN movement and assumption

Question. Cylinders 0–199, head=50, queue {10,80,150}, initial direction upward. Use SCAN; include travel to disk endpoint.

Path. 50→80→150→199→10. Movement=30+70+49+189=338 cylinders.

Reasoning/check. SCAN proceeds to the endpoint even though last request was at 150, then reverses. Requests 10 is serviced on return. If the course convention excludes endpoint movement, report that convention; typical SCAN includes it.

Challenging 1 | Scheduling trade-off

Question. Interactive short jobs coexist with long CPU-bound jobs. Compare FCFS, RR, and SJF for responsiveness and fairness.

Reasoning. FCFS is arrival-fair but a long burst creates convoy delay. SJF can reduce mean waiting when burst estimates are reliable, yet estimates are imperfect and long jobs can starve. RR bounds the time before another turn approximately by queue length×quantum, improving response, but excessive context-switching at tiny quantum lowers useful CPU time.

Answer. RR is often the best of these three for interactive responsiveness; FCFS is simplest; SJF favors low average waiting under known bursts. State that no policy wins every metric.

Challenging 2 | Producer-consumer ordering

Question. A bounded buffer has capacity N. Give a deadlock-safe producer sequence and explain why ordering matters.

Answer. Initialize empty=N, full=0, mutex=1. Producer: wait(empty); wait(mutex); insert; signal(mutex); signal(full). Consumer: wait(full); wait(mutex); remove; signal(mutex); signal(empty). Waiting for an available slot/item before taking the mutex prevents a process from holding the buffer lock while blocked on a condition that another process needs the lock to change.

Challenging 3 | Integrate address translation and fault handling

Question. Page size=1024 bytes. Logical address 2500 is referenced; page 2 is absent. Explain translation and the consequence.

Work. Page number=floor(2500/1024)=2; offset=2500−2×1024=452 bytes. Since page 2 is absent, translation cannot complete normally: a page fault traps to the OS. OS locates/loads page 2 into a frame (choosing a victim if necessary), updates the page table, then restarts the instruction. If page 2 loads into frame 7, physical address=7×1024+452=7620 bytes.

Check. Offset 452 is less than page size 1024; physical address lies within frame 7.

Challenging 4 | Choose deadlock strategy

Question. Two processes request resources in opposite orders. Give one prevention rule and distinguish prevention from avoidance.

Answer. Impose a global ordering on resource types and require requests in increasing order; this removes circular wait. Prevention disallows a necessary deadlock condition in all executions. Avoidance instead examines current allocation and maximum claims and grants a request only if the resulting state remains safe.
